\subsection{Session 13: audit of the reduction chain}\label{sec:s13}

Session 13 opened with the audit recommended in the session-11 health
note: nothing in the chain Theorem~\ref{thm:marked} $\to$
Problem~\ref{prob:annealed} $\to$ Theorem~\ref{thm:factor} $\to$
Problem~\ref{prob:mixing} $\to$ Problem~\ref{prob:survival} $\to$
(R1)+(C1)+(C2$''$) had been re-read since it was laid, and two carried
conditions had just been found false in consecutive sessions.  The
audit produced three corrections to the \emph{form} of the bottom of
the chain, none of which changes what is proved above it:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item[(1)] the quantity the programme consumes is an $L^2$ average of
the quenched parity $\Phi_m$ over the start, not a supremum over
states (Proposition~\ref{prop:S2red}); the state-uniform target (S)
of \S\ref{sec:s12left} was introduced by the strong-Markov step of
the session-10 reduction, not demanded by anything above it, and the
inert-dust obstruction of \S\ref{sec:s12dust} does not touch the
$L^2$ form (Remark~\ref{rem:dustblind});
\item[(2)] the exponent budget carried since session 10
($\gamma>4/9$ at $z=-1$, hence $p>13/5$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:holder}) is too demanding by a factor of
about five: the correct budget is $\gamma>1/11$, hence $p>6/5$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:budget}).  The measured $p\approx2.7$ and
modulus $0.75$ are far inside it, not marginal;
\item[(3)] $\tau_k<\infty$ a.s.\ from every state with $ab>0$, taken
for granted since session 10, has a four-line proof
(Lemma~\ref{lem:flipsrecur}).
\end{enumerate}
The occupation-time step and (M$_1$) are stated exactly and left open
(\S\ref{sec:s13occ}, \S\ref{sec:s13m1}).

\subsubsection{The reduction consumes an \texorpdfstring{$L^2$}{L2} average, not a supremum}
\label{sec:s13ltwo}

Recall (Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient}) that
$\psi(j,d)=\E_{\mu_j}[\Phi_d(\Gamma_0)^2]$ with
$\Phi_m(\Gamma)=\E_\Gamma[(-1)^{N_m}]$ the parity of the number of
flips in $m$ steps of the quotient chain, and that
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor} needs $\psi(j,d)\le Kd^{-2c}$ for
$j\le8d$ only.  Fix $K\ge3$ and
$S'_K=\{a,b\ge1/(16K)\}$ as in \S\ref{sec:markovrenewal}.

\begin{proposition}[$L^2$ form of the reduction to $S'_K$]\label{prop:S2red}
Let $d\ge1$, $j\le8d$, $0<\varepsilon\le1/(2K)$, and let
$T^*=T^*(\varepsilon,K,d)<d/2$ be any deterministic time.  Let
$T'=\inf\{t\ge0:\Gamma_t\in S'_K\}$ and let
$\nu=\nu_{j,\varepsilon,K,T^*}$ be the joint law of
$(\Gamma_{T'},T')$ under $\Pr_{\mu_j}(\,\cdot\,;\,g_0\ge\varepsilon,\
T'\le T^*)$, a sub-probability measure on $S'_K\times\{0,\dots,T^*\}$;
write $\nu_t=\nu(\cdot\times\{t\})$ and, when $\nu_t\ne0$, let
$\bar\nu_t=\nu_t/\nu_t(S'_K)$ be the \emph{time-$t$ entrance law}, a
probability measure on $S'_K$.  Then
\[
\E_{\mu_j}\bigl[\Phi_d(\Gamma_0)^2;\ g_0\ge\varepsilon\bigr]
\;\le\;2\int\Phi_{d-t}(x)^2\,\nu(dx,dt)
\;+\;2\,\Pr_{\mu_j}\bigl[T'>T^*;\ g_0\ge\varepsilon\bigr]
\;\le\;2\sup_{t\le T^*}\E_{\bar\nu_t}\bigl[\Phi_{d-t}^2\bigr]
\;+\;2\,\Pr_{\mu_j}\bigl[T'>T^*;\ g_0\ge\varepsilon\bigr].
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$T'$ is a stopping time and $T'\wedge T^*\le T^*<d$.  By the strong
Markov property at $T'\wedge T^*$ and the additivity of the flip
count, $\E[(-1)^{N_d-N_{T'}}\mid\mathcal F_{T'}]=\Phi_{d-T'}(\Gamma_{T'})$
on $\{T'\le T^*\}$, so
\[
\Phi_d(\Gamma_0)
=\E_{\Gamma_0}\bigl[(-1)^{N_{T'}}\Phi_{d-T'}(\Gamma_{T'});\ T'\le T^*\bigr]
+\E_{\Gamma_0}\bigl[(-1)^{N_d};\ T'>T^*\bigr]
=:A(\Gamma_0)+B(\Gamma_0).
\]
Then $|B|\le\Pr_{\Gamma_0}[T'>T^*]\le1$, so $B^2\le|B|$, and by
Cauchy--Schwarz
$A^2\le\E_{\Gamma_0}[\Phi_{d-T'}(\Gamma_{T'})^2;\,T'\le T^*]\cdot
\Pr_{\Gamma_0}[T'\le T^*]\le\E_{\Gamma_0}[\Phi_{d-T'}(\Gamma_{T'})^2;\,T'\le T^*]$.
Use $(A+B)^2\le2A^2+2B^2$ and integrate over $\Gamma_0\sim\mu_j$ on
$\{g_0\ge\varepsilon\}$; the first integral is, by definition of
$\nu$, $\int\Phi_{d-t}(x)^2\nu(dx,dt)=\sum_{t\le T^*}\nu_t(S'_K)\,
\E_{\bar\nu_t}[\Phi_{d-t}^2]$, and $\sum_t\nu_t(S'_K)\le1$.  (The
supremum over $t$ must stay inside the sum in this way: a bound in
terms of the time-marginal $\sum_t\nu_t$ alone would cost a factor
$T^*$.)
\end{proof}

\emph{Status: PROVED.  Compare the session-10 reduction paragraph at
the end of \S\ref{sec:markovrenewal}, which at the same point bounds
$|\Phi_d(\Gamma_0)|\le\E|\Phi_{d-T'}(\Gamma_{T'})|+\Pr[T'>T^*]$ and
then takes the supremum of $|\Phi_m|$ over $S'_K\cap H$.  The
supremum is where the state-uniform target (S) entered the
programme; nothing above this point asks for it.  What the programme
needs on $S'_K$ is therefore:}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(S$_2$)}\quad
$\displaystyle\E_{\bar\nu_t}\bigl[\Phi_{m}^2\bigr]\le C(K)\,m^{-2\gamma}$
\emph{for every $t\le T^*$ and $m=d-t\in[d/2,d]$, for the time-$t$
entrance laws $\bar\nu_t=\bar\nu_{j,\varepsilon,K,T^*;t}$ of $S'_K$,
$j\le8d$, with $K=K_{\gamma'}(9d)=O(\log d)$ and $C(K)$ polynomial
in $K$,}

\medskip
\noindent together with the entrance estimate
$\Pr_{\mu_j}[T'>T^*;\,g_0\ge\varepsilon]\le O(d^{-\gamma'})+O(\varepsilon K\log d)$
for $T^*=O(\varepsilon^{-1}K\log d)$, which is the content of the
session-10 reduction paragraph (Lemma~\ref{lem:srecharge} plus the
occupation-time step of \S\ref{sec:s13occ}).  The block-bound event
$H_{9d}$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:horizon} is also handled in $L^2$
without any state-by-state argument: with $H=\{B_r<K,\ r\le m\}$
for the chain from $x$, writing $\Phi_m=\Phi_m^H+\Phi_m^{H^c}$ with
$|\Phi_m^{H^c}(x)|\le\Pr_x[H^c]$, one has
$\E_{\bar\nu_t}[\Phi_m^2]\le2\E_{\bar\nu_t}[(\Phi_m^H)^2]+2\E_{\bar\nu_t}[\Pr_x[H^c]]$,
and $\sum_t\nu_t(S'_K)\,\E_{\bar\nu_t}[\Pr_x[H^c]]\le\Pr_{\mu_j}[H^c_{9d}]\le(9d+1)^{-\gamma'}$
because $\nu$ is a sub-law of the path measure from $\mu_j$, whose
clock reaches at most $j+d\le9d$.

\begin{remark}[what the dust obstruction does and does not touch]
\label{rem:dustblind}
(a) Proposition~\ref{prop:nomino} and Corollary~\ref{cor:noerg} are
statements about the supremum norm on $\Omega_K$: they show that no
Doeblin-type argument can give a bound on $\sup_{x\in S'_K}|\Phi_m(x)|$
(or on $\|\kappa_1^kF-\bar\pi F\|_\infty$ for any weight).  They say
nothing against (S$_2$), which is an average over laws $\bar\nu_t$
that are themselves marginals of the chain run from one block.
(b) Under the coupling of Lemma~\ref{lem:inert}, $\Phi_m$ is
Lipschitz in the dust: $|\Phi_m(y_\varepsilon)-\Phi_m(y)|\le4\varepsilon m$,
since the flip counts agree on the coupling event.  So $\Phi_m$ is a
continuous function in the coarse topology at scale $1/m$ --- the
topology in which $y_\varepsilon\to y$ --- and the dust of
Proposition~\ref{prop:nomino}, which lives at scales
$\varepsilon_i\ll1/T_i$, is invisible to the functions that (S$_2$)
is about.  This is the precise sense in which \S\ref{sec:s12left}(i)
asked for a ``dust-blind'' statement, and (S$_2$) is one.
(c) (S$_2$) is \emph{not} an annealed statement in the naive sense:
it bounds the second moment of the quenched parity, so a proof must
still show that $\Phi_m(x)$ is small for $\bar\nu_t$-typical $x$, with
the environment of $x$ frozen; only the \emph{uniformity} over $x$
has been removed.  The quantity $\E_{\bar\nu_t}[\Phi_m]$ (parity
averaged over the environment of the start) is not what is needed and
is not sufficient.
(d) The natural function space for (S$_2$) is $L^2(\bar\pi)$, in
which the chain is self-adjoint (\S\ref{sec:spectral}); the spectral
route of session 8, shelved in favour of the Markov-renewal route,
is the dust-blind route.  What it additionally needs is a comparison
of the entrance laws $\bar\nu_t$ with $\bar\pi$ on the functions
$\Phi_m^2$; this is the first sub-problem of \S\ref{sec:s13spectral}.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{The exponent budget, recomputed}\label{sec:s13budget}

\begin{corollary}[the budget is $\gamma>1/11$, not $\gamma>4/9$]
\label{cor:budget}
Suppose (S$_2$) holds with exponent $\gamma\in(0,\tfrac12)$ and the
entrance estimate holds as stated after Proposition~\ref{prop:S2red}.
Then $\psi(j,d)\le C'(\log d)^{O(1)}\,d^{-2\gamma}$ for all $j\le8d$,
i.e.\ Problem~\ref{prob:mixing} holds with $c=\gamma$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:psienough} closes the iid model as soon as
$\gamma>1/11$.  The same holds if (S$_2$) is replaced by the
state-uniform (S) with exponent $\gamma$.  In particular, in
Proposition~\ref{prop:holder} the crude route needs only $p>6/5$,
and (R1) needs only $C^{1,\gamma}$ regularity with $\gamma>1/11$,
i.e.\ moments $\E_x[\tau^{1+\gamma}]$ with $\gamma>1/11$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Set $\varepsilon:=d^{-1/2-\gamma}$ and $T^*:=C\varepsilon^{-1}K\log d$
with $K=K_{\gamma'}(9d)$, $\gamma'\ge2\gamma$; then
$T^*=O(d^{1/2+\gamma}\log^2d)<d/2$ for $d$ large because
$\gamma<\tfrac12$, and $\varepsilon\le1/(2K)$.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:shorthorizon} ($j\le8d$),
$\Pr_{\mu_j}[g_0<\varepsilon]\le4\varepsilon^2j\le32\varepsilon^2d=32d^{-2\gamma}$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:S2red} and (S$_2$) (with $m=d-t\ge d/2$),
$\E_{\mu_j}[\Phi_d^2;g_0\ge\varepsilon]\le2C(K)(d/2)^{-2\gamma}
+2\bigl(O(d^{-\gamma'})+O(\varepsilon K\log d)\bigr)$, and
$\varepsilon K\log d=O(d^{-1/2-\gamma}\log^2d)=o(d^{-2\gamma})$ since
$\gamma<\tfrac12$.  Add, and use $|\Phi_d|\le1$ on $\{g_0<\varepsilon\}$.
The budget: Corollary~\ref{cor:psienough} needs $c>1/11$.
For Proposition~\ref{prop:holder}: $(p-1)/(p+1)>1/11\iff p>6/5$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Status: PROVED (arithmetic).  Where the factor was lost.  The
session-10 reduction paragraph ends by bounding $|\Phi_d(\Gamma_0)|$,
not $\Phi_d(\Gamma_0)^2$, by $C(\varepsilon d)^{-2\alpha}$ --- the
form of Problem~\ref{prob:survival}, which is a bound on the

\subsubsection{Flips recur: \texorpdfstring{$\tau_k<\infty$}{tau\_k<infty} a.s.}\label{sec:s13tau}

\begin{lemma}[flips recur]\label{lem:flipsrecur}
From every state of the quotient chain with $a_0b_0>0$,
$\sum_t2a_tb_t=\infty$ a.s.; consequently flips occur infinitely
often a.s., and $\tau_k<\infty$ a.s.\ for every $k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $m_t=\min(a_t,b_t)$, $M_t=\max(a_t,b_t)$ and
$p_t=2a_tb_t=2m_tM_t$, the conditional probability of a flip at step
$t+1$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:splitmerge} (read on the quotient,
Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient}), at a non-flip step a marked coordinate
either stays fixed, increases (a merge), or is multiplied by
$\sqrt U$, $U\sim U(0,1)$ (a shrink: both cuts in that coordinate's
material, probability equal to the square of the coordinate).  Hence
$m_t$ is non-decreasing between the steps at which it strictly
decreases, and at a non-flip step $m$ can decrease in exactly two
ways: a shrink of the smaller coordinate, probability $m_t^2$; or a
shrink of the larger coordinate below $m_t$, probability
$M_t^2\cdot\Pr[M_t\sqrt U<m_t]=M_t^2(m_t/M_t)^2=m_t^2$.  So the event
$E_{t+1}=\{\text{step }t{+}1\text{ is not a flip and }m_{t+1}<m_t\}$
has $\Pr[E_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]\le2m_t^2\le2m_tM_t=p_t$.

Let $\Omega_0=\{\sum_tp_t<\infty\}$.  By L\'evy's conditional
Borel--Cantelli lemma, on $\Omega_0$ almost surely only finitely many
flips occur and only finitely many $E_t$ occur.  All marked
coordinates stay strictly positive a.s.\ ($U>0$, flips land in the
open interval), so after the last flip and the last $E_t$ the process
$m_t$ is non-decreasing and bounded below by some $m_*>0$; then
$p_t\ge2m_*^2$ for all large $t$ and $\sum_tp_t=\infty$,
contradicting $\Omega_0$.  Hence $\Pr[\Omega_0]=0$, i.e.\
$\sum_tp_t=\infty$ a.s., and L\'evy's lemma in the other direction
gives flips infinitely often a.s.
\end{proof}

\emph{Status: PROVED.  This is the hypothesis of
Propositions~\ref{prop:r2false}, \ref{prop:nomino}, \ref{prop:nodrift}
and of the remark after Proposition~\ref{prop:genfun}; it needs
nothing about the environment --- in particular it holds with an
all-dust environment --- because the only mechanism that decreases the
smaller marked coordinate has probability at most the flip
probability.  It gives no rate; rates are (R1).}

\subsubsection{The occupation-time step, stated}\label{sec:s13occ}

The session-10 reduction (end of \S\ref{sec:markovrenewal}) and the
entrance estimate after Proposition~\ref{prop:S2red} use, without
proof, the following statement.

\begin{lemma}[occupation time of the marked total; OPEN]\label{lem:occupation}
Let $K\ge3$ and let $H=H_T$ be the block-bound event with bound $K$.
There are $c_K,C_K>0$ such that for every start with
$s_0\ge1/(2K)$, every $\varepsilon\le1/(8K)$ and every $T\ge1$,
\[
\Pr\Bigl[\#\{t\le T:\ s_t<\tfrac1{4K}\}>\tfrac T2\Bigr]
\;\le\;C_K\,e^{-c_K\varepsilon T/K}\;+\;2\varepsilon^2T\;+\;\Pr[H^c].
\]
\end{lemma}

\emph{Status: OPEN; believed standard.  What it is used for: in the
entrance estimate, after $s$ first reaches $1/(2K)$ (within
$T_1=O(\varepsilon^{-1}K\log d)$ steps by Lemma~\ref{lem:srecharge}),
one needs, on at least half of the next $T_2=O(\varepsilon^{-1}K\log d)$
steps, $s_t\ge1/(4K)$ and $\min(a_t,b_t)\ge\varepsilon$ (the second
from the flux bound, cost $4\varepsilon^2T_2$); on those steps a flip
has probability $\ge2\varepsilon(s_t-\varepsilon)\ge\varepsilon/(4K)$
and lands in $S'_K$ with conditional probability $\ge\tfrac12$
(the trapezoid of Lemma~\ref{lem:recharge}(ii) is symmetric and
unimodal), so $T'\le T_1+T_2$ except with probability
$O(d^{-\gamma'})+O(\varepsilon K\log d)$.  What fails if it is false:
the entrance estimate, hence the reduction of (S$_2$) to the
log-macroscopic set $S'_K$; (S$_2$) would then have to be proved for
the laws $\mu_j$ restricted to $\{g_0\ge\varepsilon\}$ with
$\varepsilon=d^{-1/2-\gamma}$, i.e.\ with the trap accounting inside
the $L^2$ statement.  Nothing above Problem~\ref{prob:survival}
depends on it.  Intended proof: Lemma~\ref{lem:logdrift} gives
$\E[\Delta\log s\mid\Gamma_t]\ge c\,s_t/K$ on $H\cap\{s_t\le1/(2K)\}$,
with down-jumps those of $\tfrac12\log U$; so for small $\theta>0$,
$V=s^{-\theta}$ is a supermartingale on $\{s\le1/(2K)\}\cap H$ with
drift $\le-\theta c's^{1-\theta}/K$, and the occupation time of
$\{\varepsilon\le s<1/(4K)\}$ over $T$ steps is at most
$\varepsilon^{-(1-\theta)}$ times the accumulated drift; the time
below $\varepsilon$ is priced by Lemma~\ref{lem:strap}; excursions
above $1/(2K)$ are handled by stopping $V$ at $1/(2K)$.  The missing
piece is only the bookkeeping of the re-entries into
$\{s<1/(2K)\}$ from above, each of which starts $V$ at
$\le(2K)^\theta\cdot2^\theta$ (a single shrink from $\ge1/(2K)$
lands at $\ge s\sqrt U$, and $\Pr[\sqrt U\le\tfrac12]=\tfrac14$) and
of which there are at most $O(T/K^2)$ in expectation (shrinks have
probability $\le s^2$).  We have not written it out.}

\subsubsection{What (M\texorpdfstring{$_1$}{1}) needs}\label{sec:s13m1}

Proposition~\ref{prop:holder} assumes (M$_1$):
$\sup_{\bar x\in S'_K}\E_{\bar x}[\tau_k]\le C_1k$.  By the strong
Markov property at the flip times,
$\E_{\bar x}[\tau_k]=\sum_{i<k}\E_{\bar x}\bigl[\E_{\bar\Gamma_{\tau_i}}[\tau]\bigr]$,
so (M$_1$) follows from a bound on the mean first-flip time that is
uniform \emph{on average over post-flip states}.  The post-flip state
has marked total $s$ (preserved by the flip) and landing gap $g'$
with $\Pr[g'\le\eta]\le\eta^2/(ab)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:recharge}(ii)),
so $\E[1/g']\le\int_0^{s}\eta^{-2}\Pr[g'\le\eta]\,d\eta\cdot
\text{(const)}<\infty$ is automatic from a macroscopic pre-flip
state; what is needed is therefore
\[
\text{(R1$'$)}\qquad
\E_{\bar x}[\tau]\;\le\;C(K)\,\bigl(1+1/g(\bar x)\bigr)\quad
\text{on }H,\ \text{uniformly over the environment},
\]
together with control of the pre-flip state at each flip (so that
$1/(ab)$ in the landing bound is $O(K^2)$ on average), which is the
recurrence of the marked pair to $S'_K$ --- the same recharge
machinery as the entrance estimate.  (R1$'$) is the first-moment
part of TODO~11a (which asks for the tail $\Pr_x[\tau>t]\le C_Kt^{-2}$,
measured in \S\ref{sec:parity}(c)); by Corollary~\ref{cor:budget},
(R1) itself now needs only a $(1+\gamma)$-th moment with
$\gamma>1/11$, so a tail bound $t^{-1-\delta}$ for any $\delta>1/11$
would suffice for both.  (M$_p$) in Corollary~\ref{cor:noerg}(ii) is
used only in a negative result and needs no further attention.

\subsubsection{Where this leaves the programme}\label{sec:s13spectral}

Items (I)--(III) of the overview are unchanged in substance, but (I)
is now: prove (S$_2$) with any $\gamma>1/11$, for the time-$t$
entrance laws $\bar\nu_t$ of $S'_K$, with $C(K)$ polynomial.  Two routes remain, neither of
which is Doeblin-type:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(i)] \emph{The spectral route} (\S\ref{sec:spectral}): the
quotient chain is reversible for $\bar\pi$; for the odd function
$f=\varepsilon F$ the Dirichlet form has the massive flip term, and
polynomial decay of $\psi_{\bar\pi}(d)=\|P^d\varepsilon\|^2_{L^2(\pi)}$
with exponent $\beta$ is equivalent to the spectral measure of
$\varepsilon$ having mass $O(\delta^\beta)$ within $\delta$ of
$\pm1$.  A sufficient functional inequality is
\[
\text{(H$_\beta$)}\qquad
\bigl|\E_{\bar\pi}[F]\bigr|^2\;\le\;C\,\mathcal E_{\mathrm{odd}}(F)^{\beta}\,\|F\|_{L^2(\bar\pi)}^{2(1-\beta)}
\qquad\text{for all }F\in L^2(\bar\pi),
\]
with $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{odd}}(F)=\tfrac12\E_{\bar\pi}[(F-F')^2;\text{non-flip}]
+\tfrac12\E_{\bar\pi}[(F+F')^2;\text{flip}]$: applied to
$F=\Pi_\delta\varepsilon$ (spectral projection onto
$[1-\delta,1]$) it gives $\nu_\varepsilon([1-\delta,1])\le C\delta^\beta$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}, $\beta\le1$; the budget needs only
$\beta>2/11$.  (The limiting case $\beta=1$ is a Hardy inequality
in the gap coordinate and is false by a logarithm: test functions
$F=g^{-1}\log^{-2}(1/g)\mathbf 1\{g\le\delta\}$ have
$|\E F|^2/\mathcal E_{\mathrm{odd}}(F)\asymp\log(1/\delta)$ --- consistent
with $\psi_{\bar\pi}\asymp1/d$, since linear spectral density gives
$\int d\nu_\varepsilon/(1-\lambda)=\infty$.)  The periodicity
caveat of \S\ref{sec:spectral} applies: the same inequality is
needed for $(-1)^BF$, i.e.\ near $\lambda=-1$.  What the spectral
route does \emph{not} give by itself is (S$_2$) for the entrance
laws $\bar\nu_t$: these are singular with respect to $\bar\pi$
(finitely many blocks against infinitely many), and the comparison
must be made at a coarse scale --- the blocks of mass $\gtrsim1/(m\log m)$,
which are the only ones $\Phi_m$ sees (Remark~\ref{rem:dustblind}(b))
--- where $\bar\nu_t$ has had time of order $m\log m\gg$ the
relaxation time $1/(2\eta)$ of each scale $\eta$ in that range.
This start-comparison is the first concrete sub-problem.
\item[(ii)] \emph{The exact-law route} of \S\ref{sec:s12left}(iv):
polynomial decay of the block-count parity at flip times from the
exact fixed-time laws, from the one-curve start, extended to the
$\mu_j$ starts.  Its target is now $p>6/5$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:holder}, i.e.\ $a_k=O(k^{-6/5})$, far weaker
than the measured $k^{-3}$.  Since $B(\bar\Gamma_{\tau_k})$ spreads
only like $\log k$, the decay cannot come from the width of its law;
it comes from the smoothness of the law of $\tau_k$ on the time axis
(the parity of $B$ at a \emph{fixed} time is deterministic,
Lemma~\ref{lem:parity}), and quantifying that smoothness is exactly
what (ii) must do.
\end{itemize}
Both routes consume the same two inputs that the audit has left open
--- the occupation-time lemma and (R1$'$) --- and both are now aimed at
a target with a fivefold margin.

\subsubsection{Scouting (III): Problem~\ref{prob:decouple} is not needed}
\label{sec:s13decouple}

Problem~\ref{prob:decouple} asks that the fraction of marks whose
column lacks intercalate supply be $o(1/\log n)$ \emph{uniformly over
all admissible} $(\lambda,\alpha,\beta)$, including merges whose
$m$-cycle class occupies $O(1)$ columns.  But
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} uses $\mathrm{EQ}$ only along its routing
chains, and on those chains the merged part is always large.

\begin{proposition}[no decoupling is needed on the routing chains]
\label{prop:nodecouple}
Let $K=\lceil A\log n\rceil$ be as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}.  Every split $\lambda\to\mu$ used by its
routing chains (for plain types with $\kappa(\lambda)\le K$ parts)
merges a part $m\ge n/K$.  Consequently, on the intercalate-expander
event, the number of marks $(L',P)$ of $X(n,\lambda;\alpha,\beta)$
whose column $j$ fails the supply property of Lemma~\ref{lem:supply}
is at most $14\log^{22}n$ per square, out of $\lambda_mm\ge n/K$
marks per square: the exceptional-mark fraction is at most
$14A\log^{23}n/n=n^{-1+o(1)}$, for every chain and every square.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The routing of Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} builds the chain in
reverse by splitting parts off a residual $R$ smallest-first; the
part merged at each step of the forward chain is the residual $R$ at
that step.  In the case ``two largest parts unequal'', $R$ always
contains the largest part $p_{\max}\ge n/\kappa\ge n/K$; in the case
``two largest parts equal to $\ell_{\max}$'', the first split has
$R=n$ and every later residual contains the reserved copy of
$\ell_{\max}\ge n/\kappa$.  So $m=R\ge n/K$, and the $m$-class has
$\lambda_mm\ge m\ge n/K$ columns.

For the count: apply Lemma~\ref{lem:supply} \emph{without} the
constraint ``avoid column $j'$'' (drop $j'$ from its hypotheses; the
proof is unchanged), with $\ell=\log^{11}n$, $t=6\ell+1$: all but
fewer than $t\ell<7\log^{22}n$ columns $c$ carry $t$ stable
intercalates avoiding rows $1,2$, pairwise disjoint in rows, partner
columns and symbols.  For a mark $P=\{j,j'\}$ with $j$ outside this
exceptional set $E$, at most one of the $t$ intercalates through $j$
has partner column $j'$ (the partner columns are distinct), and
discarding it leaves $6\ell$ useful ones avoiding $j'$: the mark is
good.  So a bad mark has a column in $E$.  For $\alpha\ne\beta$ the
$m$ marks $\{j,\sigma^\alpha(j)\}$ of an $m$-cycle are distinct
(coincidence would need $\sigma^{2\alpha}(j)=j$, i.e.\ $m\mid2\alpha$,
impossible for $0<\alpha<m$, $2\alpha\ne m$) and each column of the
cycle lies in exactly two of them; hence at most $2|E|<14\log^{22}n$
marks are bad.  (For $\alpha=\beta$, which the routing avoids, each
column lies in one mark and the bound is $|E|$.)  With $t=6\ell+1$
in place of $6\ell$ the hypothesis $4t\ell+2t+\ell+2\le\beta n/2$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:supply} still holds for large $n$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Status: PROVED.  Consequence: the exceptional-mark bookkeeping
of Remark~\ref{rem:bookkeeping}(i) is closed for the purposes of
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction}, with room $n^{1-o(1)}$ to spare, and
Problem~\ref{prob:decouple} can be struck from the programme.  The
types with small $m$-classes --- the ones on which the deterministic
bound is vacuous and on which Problem~\ref{prob:decouple} is a
genuine question --- are exactly those the routing never uses, or
reaches only through a large merge.  (The heuristic in
\S\ref{sec:vacuity}, that class membership is a rows-$1,2$ property,
is in any case not right: the cycle type of $\sigma$ is determined by
the bottom rectangle $L_\downarrow$ alone, since a leftover cycle of
length $2\ell$ of the missing-pair graph contributes an $\ell$-cycle
of $\sigma$ whichever way it is oriented; the orientations are fair
coins that change nothing.  So the question was never about rows
$1,2$ against rows $3..n$; but it does not need an answer.)}

\subsubsection{Scouting (II): the transfer from the iid model to the orbit measure}
\label{sec:s13transfer}

Item (II) was carried since session 7 as ``transfer problem 7a(i)''
and reformulated in \S\ref{sec:transfer} as (a) an
exchangeable-to-mixture step plus (b) a $\theta$-covariant mixing
bound.  Reading Theorem~\ref{thm:master} against
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor} with fresh eyes gives a different
classification.  What the true orbit supplies is: a \emph{frame}
$\pi_0=\pi_P(L_0)$, a permutation of the rows whose cycles are the
curves; the marked points $u,v=$ rows $1,2$; and a \emph{pool} of $k$
chords, the row pairs of the useful stable intercalates, in positions
determined by $L_0$.  The iid model replaces the frame by a single
circle with $u,v$ antipodal and the pool by iid uniform chords.  Three
separate statements are therefore being modelled, of very different
status.

\paragraph{(T3) The start is stationary, not one-block.}
The frame is the cycle structure of the pair permutation of two
columns of a random Latin square with two marked rows: in the
continuum it is the stationary law $\pi$ of the marked chain
(Proposition~\ref{prop:gaplawstat}), not the one-curve state.  Two
things follow.  First, the ``free'' short-horizon inputs of
\S\ref{sec:s9}--\ref{sec:s10} --- Proposition~\ref{prop:shorthorizon}
(gap law $4\varepsilon^2j$) and Corollary~\ref{cor:horizon} (block
count $O(\log d)$ from the one-block start) --- are artefacts of the
model.  Under the true start the gap law is the linear stationary
one, $\Pr[g_0\le\varepsilon]\asymp4\varepsilon$, so the decomposition
of \S\ref{sec:upper} gives $\psi\lesssim\varepsilon+C(K)(d-T^*)^{-2\gamma}$
with $T^*=\varepsilon^{-1}\,\mathrm{polylog}$, i.e.\
$\psi\lesssim d^{-\min(1,2\gamma)}\,\mathrm{polylog}$: the budget
$c>1/11$ is \emph{unaffected} (and the trap term reproduces the true
rate $1/d$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:lower}).  The block bound is
replaced by the stationary one: blocks of mass $\ge\eta$ number
$O(\log(1/\eta))$ with exponential tails under $\mathrm{PD}(1)$, and
blocks below $\eta=1/(k\log^2n)$ carry total mass $\asymp\eta$ and are
inert over the horizon (Lemma~\ref{lem:inert}: hit with probability
$\le2k\eta=o(1/\log n)$).  Second, for the spectral route of
\S\ref{sec:s13spectral} this is good news: the start-comparison
sub-problem disappears, because the quantity the true ensemble needs
is $\psi_\pi(d)$ itself, coarse-grained.  (S$_2$) and the entrance
estimate of \S\ref{sec:s13ltwo} are posed for the entrance laws of
the one-block-started $\mu_j$ and must be re-posed for the
stationary start; the arithmetic above is what that re-posing
costs.  Note also that ``the frame is stationary'' is itself a
statement about $\mathrm{Unif}(S(\lambda))$ and the special pair
$(j,\sigma^\alpha(j))$ --- it is (T1) below, and indeed
$\Pr[u\sim v]=\tfrac12$ for the frame is the weak conjecture's own
bit, since the base point of an orbit is a uniform orbit point; the
mixing argument needs only the frame's \emph{health}, not its
exact law.

\paragraph{(T1) Health of the frame under the orbit measure.}
The chain analysis needs the frame to have $O(\log k)$ blocks above
scale $1/(k\log^2n)$ and $u,v$ untrapped except on a set of marks of
measure $O(\mathrm{polylog}/k)$ --- properties that a uniform random
permutation has.  The frame is the pair permutation of the columns
$j,\sigma^\alpha(j)$ of $L\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S(\lambda))$: by
transposition symmetry its cycle type has, for a \emph{uniform}
square and a \emph{uniform} column pair, exactly the law $\PP_n$
--- the object of the conjecture.  What is needed is far weaker (a
polylogarithmic block count at coarse scale, a trapped fraction
$O(1/k)$), but it is needed under the conditioning on $S(\lambda)$
and for the special pair $(j,\sigma^\alpha(j))$, and it cannot be
imported from the uniform measure by the union-of-costs device of
Lemma~\ref{lem:condexp}: the device transfers only properties whose
failure probability is $e^{-\omega(n\log^2n)}$, while ``more than
$2n/k$ marks are trapped'' fails with probability of order
$e^{-n/k}$ at best.  Status: UNEXAMINED; no proof route visible
beyond the one sketched under (T2).

\paragraph{(T2) Genericity of the pool.}
This is the real content of (II).  Step (a) of \S\ref{sec:transfer}
is routine: take the supplied family through $j$ with
$t\asymp n/\log^{11}n$ members (Lemma~\ref{lem:supply} allows
$t\le\beta n/(8\ell)$) and use a uniformly random $k$-subset of it as
the pool; by Diaconis--Freedman the $k$ chords are, conditionally on
$L$, iid from the empirical pair law $\theta_L$ of the family up to
total variation $k^2/(2t)=n^{-1+o(1)}$, and the hard-core correction
is absent because the family is already pairwise disjoint.  Step (b)
is \emph{not} a mixing statement: the chain analysis is covariant
under reparametrisation of the circle by an atomless measure $m$
only when the two endpoints of a chord are \emph{independent} draws
from $m$; $\theta_L$ is an empirical law on \emph{pairs} of frame
positions, and if (say) the intercalate rows $r,r'$ were always
adjacent in the cycles of $\pi_0$ the dynamics would be degenerate
and the bit frozen.  What is needed is a coarse product structure:
at scale $\eta=1/(k\log^2n)$, the fraction of the family with
$(\mathrm{pos}\,r,\mathrm{pos}\,r')\in A\times B$ is $|A||B|$ up to
$\delta\ll\eta^2/k$ for all arcs $A,B$ (a coupling of the
$\theta_L$-chain with the uniform chain over $k$ steps then costs
$O(k\delta/\eta^2)$).  Quantitatively this is mild --- a family of
$n^{1-o(1)}$ random-like points in the square has discrepancy
$n^{-1/2+o(1)}\ll\eta^2/k=\log^{-O(1)}n$ --- but it is a
\emph{distributional} statement about where the stable intercalates
of column $j$ sit relative to the pair permutation of columns
$j,j'$, under $\mathrm{Unif}(S(\lambda))$, and like (T1) it is out
of reach of union-of-costs.  The only route visible is the one
\cite{KPS25} use for the expander itself: demand the property for
all but $\ell=\log^{11}n$ columns, so that failure requires $\ell$
simultaneous failures and costs $e^{-\ell\cdot n\delta^2/\mathrm{polylog}}
=e^{-\omega(n\log^2n)}$ \emph{if} the per-column failures decouple
in the triangle-removal model --- a KPS-scale sub-project (their
Lemma~8.3 machinery re-run for a two-column pair permutation), not a
problem of the continuum chain.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Classification.}  (III): \emph{closed}
(Proposition~\ref{prop:nodecouple}).  (II)(a): routine.  (T3):
routine arithmetic given (T1), and it improves the position of (I).  (II)(T1)--(T2): \emph{hard}, of
a different nature from (I): they need distributional control of the
intercalate structure relative to a pair permutation under the
type-conditioned measure, which the existing toolkit (existence
statements transferred by union of costs) cannot give; the
plausible route is a KPS-style ``all but $\ell$ columns'' robustness
argument in the triangle-removal model.  The numerics say the
statements are true with a comfortable margin.  First concrete
sub-problem: state the minimal coarse product property of (T2)
precisely and determine whether its ``all but $\ell$ columns''
version has failure probability $e^{-\omega(n\log^2n)}$ in the
triangle-removal process; a positive answer would also deliver (T1)
by the same device.  Second: redo the bookkeeping of
\S\ref{sec:s9}--\S\ref{sec:s10} from the stationary start (T3),
which is the honest form of (I).

\subsubsection{The transfer (II): exact conditional structure, and the real-square test}
\label{sec:s13transfer2}

With \S\ref{sec:spectralexact} in hand, the whole programme rests on
(T1)--(T2).  This subsection makes them precise and tests them.

\paragraph{The conditional structure.}  Fix the mark $P=\{j,j'\}$
and write $L_{\mathrm{rest}}$ for the $n\times(n-2)$ Latin rectangle
obtained by deleting columns $j,j'$ from $L$; for each row $r$ let
$\mathrm{miss}(r)$ be the pair of symbols absent from row $r$ of
$L_{\mathrm{rest}}$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item[(i)] \emph{The frame is a function of $L_{\mathrm{rest}}$.}
$\{L(r,j),L(r,j')\}=\mathrm{miss}(r)$, and $\pi_P(r)$ is the row $r'$
with $L(r',j')=L(r,j)$, i.e.\ the other row whose missing pair contains
$L(r,j)$.  So the cycles of $\pi_P$ are exactly the cycles of the
$2$-regular ``leftover'' graph $G$ on rows ($r\sim r'$ iff
$\mathrm{miss}(r)\cap\mathrm{miss}(r')\ne\emptyset$), traversed in a
direction fixed by the orientation of each leftover cycle; the
orientations are the $2^{\#\mathrm{cycles}}$ completions of
$L_{\mathrm{rest}}$, fair coins except on the (at most two) cycles
through rows $1,2$, which the conditioning on $S(\lambda)$ and the mark
constrain.  The bit $[u\sim v]$ does not depend on the orientations.
\item[(ii)] \emph{The pool is a coin-flip thinning of a candidate set
determined by $L_{\mathrm{rest}}$.}  An intercalate $(r,r',j,c)$ with
$c\notin\{j,j'\}$ requires $L(r',c)=L(r,j)\in\mathrm{miss}(r)$ and
$L(r,c)=L(r',j)\in\mathrm{miss}(r')$.  Call $(r,r',c)$ a
\emph{candidate} if $L(r',c)\in\mathrm{miss}(r)$ and
$L(r,c)\in\mathrm{miss}(r')$ (a property of $L_{\mathrm{rest}}$).  A
candidate is an intercalate through $j$ iff both rows place the
relevant symbol in column $j$, through $j'$ iff both place it in $j'$,
and otherwise it is nothing; so it is realised on the $j$-side with
probability $\tfrac14$ and on the $j'$-side with probability $\tfrac14$
when $r,r'$ lie on different leftover cycles, and with probabilities
$(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ or $(0,0)$ when they lie on the same cycle.  The
conjugating coordinates ($c=j'$) are exactly the $2$-cycles of $\pi_P$.
Each row has about four candidate partners (a missing symbol of $r$
sits in row $r'$ in a unique column $c$, and $L(r,c)$ must then be
missing from $r'$, probability $\approx2/n$ for each of the $n-2$ rows
$r'$ and each of the two missing symbols), so there are $\approx2n$
candidates and $\approx n/2$ pool chords on each side, as observed.
\item[(iii)] \emph{Local exclusion.}  A non-conjugating pool chord
$(r,r')$ never has $\pi_P(r)=r'$ or $\pi_P(r')=r$: $L(r',c)=L(r,j)$ and
$L(r',j')=L(r,j)$ would put one symbol twice in row $r'$.  Rows
adjacent along a cycle of $\pi_P$ therefore never form a chord, and in
particular no chord has both ends in a cycle of length $\le3$ (a
$2$-cycle is conjugating, a $3$-cycle has all its pairs adjacent).
\end{enumerate}
Thus (T1)--(T2) are statements about $L_{\mathrm{rest}}$ alone: the
cycle structure of its leftover graph $G$, and the position of the
candidate graph $H$ ($r\sim r'$ iff $(r,r',c)$ is a candidate for some
$c$) relative to the cycles of $G$.

\paragraph{The test.}  \texttt{s13\_real.py} takes uniform squares
from the Jacobson--Matthews sampler, samples marks
$(j,\sigma^\alpha(j))$ with $\alpha\ge2$ in $\sigma$-cycles of length
$\ge4$, builds the frame $\pi_P$ and a randomised greedy cell-disjoint
pool of all intercalates through $j$ or $j'$ in rows $3..n$ (so
$k\approx n/2$, no cap), computes the bit as
$[u\sim v\text{ in }M_\omega\pi_P R_\omega]$ (verified against the
switched square on $60$ random subsets: $0$ mismatches), and measures,
with independent uniform subsets $S,T$ and uniform $\omega$:
$\E[\varepsilon_S\varepsilon_T]$; the level statistics
$\E[\varepsilon(\omega)\varepsilon(\omega\triangle D)]$ for
$|D|=1,2,3$ non-conjugating coordinates (the model predicts
$\langle\varepsilon,P^{|D|}\varepsilon\rangle$ up to $O(k^2/n)$); the
frame ($\#$cycles, $\Pr[u\sim v]$, $|C_u|$); and the pool's position
relative to the frame (number of chords with both ends in one
$\pi_P$-cycle against the null $\sum_i|C_{r_i}\setminus\{r_i,u,v\}|/(n-3)$,
and the relative position $d(r,r')/|C_r|$ of the second end in four
bins).  For every real instance a \emph{matched model instance} is
generated: a uniform derangement frame and uniform disjoint chords on
rows $3..n$ with the same side counts $(|\mathcal I_j|,|\mathcal I_{j'}|,|\mathcal I_{jj'}|)$,
and the same statistics are computed.

\paragraph{What is now needed, restated.}  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:stationarymodel} the programme needs, for
$L\sim\mathrm{Unif}(S(\lambda))$ on the expander event and the marks of
Theorem~\ref{thm:marked}:
\[
\text{(T)}\qquad
\E\bigl[\varepsilon(\alpha)\,\varepsilon(\alpha\rho_D)\bigr]=o(1/\log^2n),
\qquad \alpha=\pi_P(L),\ D\text{ a uniform subset of the pool},
\]
and the model says the left side is $\approx2/k'$.  Two remarks
change the shape of the problem.  (a) \emph{Large pools.}  Nothing
forces $k'=\Theta(\log^{11}n)$: Lemma~\ref{lem:supply} supplies
$t\le\beta n/(8\ell)$ intercalates per column, and the real pool has
$\approx n/2$.  With $k'=n/\mathrm{polylog}$ the trapped marks are
those with row $1$ or $2$ in a $\pi_P$-cycle of length
$\le\mathrm{polylog}\,n$, so (T1) becomes a statement about
\emph{short} column cycles of a random Latin square ---
$\E[C_\ell(\pi_P)]\le C/\ell$ for $\ell\le\mathrm{polylog}\,n$ --- rather
than about cycles of length $n/\mathrm{polylog}$; for bounded $\ell$
this is the Poisson statistics of short cycles of two columns, which
is classical for uniform squares.  (b) \emph{What union-of-costs
cannot do.}  Neither (T1) nor (T2) is an $e^{-\omega(n\log^2n)}$-robust
property, so neither transfers from the uniform measure to
$\mathrm{Unif}(S(\lambda))$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:condexp}; the natural
tool is a switching argument on $L_{\mathrm{rest}}$ that preserves
$S(\lambda)$ (switches avoiding rows $1,2$ preserve $\sigma$, the
leftover graph $G$, and hence the frame, while moving the candidate
graph $H$), i.e.\ a proof that, conditionally on $G$, the candidate
graph is in generic position.  This is the open problem of the
project; it is a problem about $n\times(n-2)$ Latin rectangles, and
the numerics say its conclusion holds with margin.

\paragraph{First concrete sub-problems.}  (1) \emph{(T1) at polylog
scale by join switchings.}  Kwan--Sudakov \cite[Thm.~3 and Fact~10]{KS18}
bound the expected number of $2$-cycles of two rows of a uniform Latin
square by $1+o(1)$ with a switching (the CGW flip joining a $2$-cycle to
another cycle, with the ``turn'' to make the pair flippable), counting
the ways in and out.  The same switching applied to a column pair
$(j,j')$ joins the cycle through row $1$ to a cycle avoiding rows
$1,2$; it changes $L(1,j),L(1,j')$ and hence $\sigma$, but only by a
merge or split of $\sigma$-cycles whose effect on $|S(\lambda)|$ is
controlled by Theorem~\ref{thm:marked} within a factor $[\tfrac12,\tfrac32]$.
Target: $\E_{\mathrm{Unif}(X)}\bigl[\#\{\text{rows in }\pi_P\text{-cycles of length}\le\ell\}\bigr]\le C\ell$
uniformly for $\ell\le n^{1/2}$ and all admissible marks, which gives
$\Pr[\text{trapped}]\le C\ell/n$ --- far more than the $o(1/\log n)$
needed, with constant-factor losses affordable.  (2) \emph{(T2) as a
switching statement on $L_{\mathrm{rest}}$.}  For a window of
relative positions $W\subset(0,1)$ along a leftover cycle, the number
of candidates $(r,r')$ with $r'$ in the window of $r$'s cycle, against
the null $|W|\cdot(\text{cycle length})\cdot(\text{candidate degree})/n$:
a switching that moves one end of a candidate (exchange the symbol
$L(r',c)$ with another occurrence by an intercalate switch in
$L_{\mathrm{rest}}$) should show the ratio is $1+O(1/\text{cycle length})$.
What the final argument needs is then
$\E_\alpha\bigl|\E[\varepsilon(\alpha\rho_D)\mid\alpha]-(P^{M}\varepsilon)(\alpha)\bigr|=o(1/\log^2n)$,
a statement comparing the conditional law of the pool given the frame
with iid chords through the single test function $\varepsilon$.

\subsubsection{(T1) is a switching lemma: trapped marks are rare}
\label{sec:s13trapped}

Fix $n$, a plain type $\lambda$ and a split $m=\alpha+\beta$ with
$\alpha\ne\beta$, and let $(L,P)$ be uniform on
$X=X(n,\lambda;\alpha,\beta)$, $P=\{j,j'\}$.  Write $\pi=\pi_P$ for
the frame (a derangement of the rows), $C_r$ for the $\pi$-cycle of a
row $r$.  Two elementary moves on Latin squares are used; both are
involutions.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(\textsc{trade})] For rows $x\ne y$ let $\rho_{x,y}$ be the
permutation of the columns with $L(y,\rho_{x,y}(q))=L(x,q)$.  For a
cycle $Q$ of $\rho_{x,y}$, exchanging the entries of rows $x$ and $y$
in the columns of $Q$ gives a Latin square (the two rows carry the
same symbols on $Q$).  If $j'\in Q\not\ni j$ the new frame is
$(x\,y)\circ\pi$; if $j\in Q\not\ni j'$ it is $\pi\circ(x\,y)$; if
both or neither lie in $Q$ the cycle partition of $\pi$ is unchanged.
Call $(x,y)$ \emph{flippable} if $j,j'$ lie in different cycles of
$\rho_{x,y}$, and let $Q_{x,y}$ be the cycle through $j'$.
\item[(\textsc{turn})] For a cycle $c$ of $\pi$, exchanging the
entries of columns $j$ and $j'$ in the rows of $c$ gives a Latin
square; the frame is inverted on $c$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:cycletrade}) and
its cycle partition is unchanged.  If $x\in c\not\ni y$, the turn
replaces $\rho_{x,y}$ by $\rho_{x,y}\circ(j\,j')$ (if instead
$y\in c\not\ni x$, by $(j\,j')\circ\rho_{x,y}$), so it toggles the
flippability of $(x,y)$.
\end{itemize}
A trade at rows $x,y\notin\{1,2\}$ leaves rows $1,2$, hence $\sigma$
and the mark, untouched.  A turn of a cycle $c\not\ni1,2$ likewise.
A turn of $C_1$ when $2\notin C_1$ replaces $\sigma$ by
$\sigma\circ(j\,j')$ (reading $j,j'$ as the row-$1$ symbols), which
splits the $m$-cycle of $\sigma$ through $j$ into cycles of lengths
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ and sends $(L,P)$ into the joining space
$J=J(n,\mu;\alpha,\beta)$ of Definition~\ref{def:marked}; a turn of a
cycle containing both rows $1$ and $2$ leaves $\sigma$ unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[short frame cycles through a marked row are rare]
\label{prop:trapped}
For $(L,P)$ uniform on $X$ and every $2\le\ell\le n/2$,
\[
\Pr\bigl[|C_1|=\ell\bigr]\;\le\;\frac{18}{n-\ell-1},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\Pr\bigl[|C_1|\le \ell\bigr]\;\le\;\frac{36\,\ell}{n}\quad(\ell\le n/2-1),
\]
and the same bounds hold for $C_2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $E_\ell=\{(L,P)\in X:\ |C_1|=\ell\}$.  (The case $C_1=\{1,2\}$
cannot occur: it would mean $\sigma(j)=j'$ and $\sigma(j')=j$, but
$j'=\sigma^\alpha(j)$ lies in an $m$-cycle with $m\ge4$.)

\emph{Forward moves.}  From $(L,P)\in E_\ell$ choose $y\in C_1\setminus\{1,2\}$
and $x\notin C_1\cup\{2\}$: at least $(\ell-2)(n-\ell-1)$ pairs
($(\ell-1)(n-\ell-1)$ if $2\notin C_1$; for $\ell=2$ this is $n-3$).
If $(x,y)$ is flippable, trade $Q_{x,y}$; otherwise turn $C_1$ (which
makes $(x,y)$ flippable, as $y\in C_1\not\ni x$) and then trade
$Q_{x,y}$ in the turned square.  In both cases the resulting frame is
$(x\,y)\circ\pi^\pm$ with $\pi^\pm$ equal to $\pi$ or to $\pi$
inverted on $C_1$, so the cycle of row $1$ becomes $C_1\cup C_x$, of
length $\ell+|C_x|>\ell$; rows $1,2$ are untouched by the trade, and
the result is in $X$ (no turn, or $2\in C_1$) or in $J$ (turn with
$2\notin C_1$).

\emph{Backward moves.}  A square $(L',P)\in X\cup J$ is reached from
$(L,P)\in E_\ell$ by the move at $(x,y)$ only if $x,y$ lie in the cycle
$C'_1$ of row $1$ in $L'$, and undoing the trade (the same trade, since
$\rho'_{x,y}=\rho_{x,y}^{-1}$ on $Q_{x,y}$) splits $C'_1$ into the arcs
$\{\pi'(y),\dots,x\}$ and $\{\pi'(x),\dots,y\}$, of which the one
containing row $1$ must have length exactly $\ell$: given $L'$, that
arc is determined by its position relative to row $1$ ($\ell$
choices), and the arc determines $\{x,y\}$.  With the binary choice
``turn back or not'' this gives at most $2\ell$ preimages per $(L',P)$.

\emph{Counting.}  The map $((L,P),\text{move})\mapsto((L',P),\text{inverse move})$
is injective, so
$|E_\ell|\,(\ell-2)(n-\ell-1)\le2\ell\,(|X|+|J|)\le6\ell\,|X|$, using
$|J|=2|X_A|\le2|X|$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:marked}).  For $\ell\ge3$ this
is the claim; for $\ell=2$ the forward count is $n-3$ and the backward
count $4$, giving $12/(n-3)$.  The bound for $C_2$ is proved by the
same argument with the roles of rows $1$ and $2$ exchanged: a turn of
$C_2\not\ni1$ replaces $\sigma$ by $(\sigma(j)\,\sigma(j'))\circ\sigma$,
which also splits the $m$-cycle into lengths $\alpha,\beta$ and lands
in $J$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Status: PROVED (modulo the referee pass noted below).  This is
the trapping half of (T1): with a pool of $k'$ chords the marks for
which the chain cannot move row $1$ or row $2$ within the horizon are
those with $|C_1|$ or $|C_2|$ below $\asymp n/k'$, of total probability
$O(1/k')$ --- exactly as for a uniform frame, up to the constant $36$.
The proof is a Kwan--Sudakov style switching \cite{KS18} in which the
only non-trivial step, the turn through row $1$, is priced by the
marked-pair identity itself: the turn is the CGW switch at the mark,
and $|J|\le2|X|$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:marked}.  It uses nothing about
intercalates.  A VERIFICATION on real squares is in
\S\ref{sec:s13transfer2} ($\Pr[|C_1|\le10]$ at $n=100$: $0.089$
real against $0.083$ model).}

\subsubsection{A third route: the trade chain}\label{sec:s13tradechain}

The moves of \S\ref{sec:s13trapped} suggest replacing the intercalate
orbit of \cite{KPS25} altogether.  Consider the Markov chain $T$ on
$X$ which, from $(L,P)$, with probability $\tfrac12$ picks a uniform
pair of rows $x\ne y$ in $\{3,\dots,n\}$ and trades $Q_{x,y}$ if
$(x,y)$ is flippable (else does nothing), and with probability
$\tfrac12$ picks a uniform row $x\in\{3,\dots,n\}$ and turns $C_x$ if
$C_x\not\ni1,2$ (else does nothing).  Every move is an involution,
the probability of a move equals that of its inverse (flippability
and $|C_x|$ are preserved), so $T$ is symmetric and
$\mathrm{Unif}(X)$ is stationary; $T$ never touches rows $1,2$, so it
preserves $S(\lambda)$ and the mark exactly, with no expander
hypothesis, no stable families and no orbit bookkeeping.  Along $T$
the frame evolves as $\pi\mapsto(x\,y)\circ\pi$ at the flippable
trades (turns do not change the bit), i.e.\ as the random-transposition
walk \emph{thinned by flippability}, and the marked bit is
$\varepsilon(\pi)$.  Hence, by stationarity, for every $M$,
\[
\E_X[\varepsilon]=\E_X\bigl[(T^M\varepsilon)(L,P)\bigr],
\qquad
(T^M\varepsilon)(L,P)=\E\bigl[\varepsilon(\pi_P(L_M))\,\big|\,L_0=L\bigr],
\]
and EQ$(\delta)$ follows from $\E_X|T^M\varepsilon|\le\delta$ for some
$M$.  If every pair were flippable, $T^M\varepsilon$ would be the
one-point function $P^M\varepsilon$ of the walk with transpositions
avoiding $u,v$, and $\sup_\alpha|(P^M\varepsilon)(\alpha)|\le2\max_\alpha
d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\text{law of }\sigma_M\mid\sigma_0=\alpha,\ \mathrm{Unif})\le2n^{-1/2}$
at $M=n\log n$ by Diaconis--Shahshahani --- EQ$(2n^{-1/2})$ with no
knowledge of the frame law, no spectral measure, and no genericity of
anything.  The entire difficulty is the thinning: which pairs are
flippable depends on $L$, so the frame is not Markov by itself.  What
is needed is that, for $\mathrm{Unif}(X)$-typical squares, the
flippable graph on $\{3,\dots,n\}$ is dense and expanding and only
locally correlated with the frame (an adjacent pair $\pi(x)=y$ is
never flippable, by \S\ref{sec:s13transfer2}(iii)); stationarity then
makes it typical at every time, and a comparison of the thinned walk
with the full one (Aldous' spectral-gap theorem for the interchange
process on a dense expander, or a direct path argument) would give
$T^M\varepsilon\to0$.  This is a second, independent formulation of
(II), with the advantage that its hypotheses are about pairs of rows
of a random Latin square --- objects the switching method of
Proposition~\ref{prop:trapped} can reach --- rather than about the
joint position of intercalates.  Numerical test: run $T$ on real
squares and compare the autocorrelation of the bit, as a function of
the number of trades performed, with $\langle\varepsilon,P^m\varepsilon\rangle$
of Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}; and measure the flippable fraction and
its correlation with the frame.

\paragraph{The trade chain, continued: what a proof needs.}  Three
observations sharpen the route.

(i) \emph{EQ from the autocorrelation.}  Since $T$ is symmetric,
$\E_X[\varepsilon(L_0)\varepsilon(L_{2M})]=\|T^M\varepsilon\|^2_{L^2(X)}\ge\langle\varepsilon,1\rangle^2=(\E_X\varepsilon)^2$.
So $\mathrm{EQ}(\delta)$ follows from
$\E_X[\varepsilon(L_0)\varepsilon(L_{2M})]\le\delta^2$ for a single $M$ ---
a stationary two-time statement, the exact analogue for the trade chain
of Corollary~\ref{cor:stationarydecay} for the walk, and the quantity
\texttt{s13\_trade.py} measures.

(ii) \emph{Which frame moves are free.}  Combining a turn with a trade
makes the transposition $(x\,y)$ available at any pair for which the
cycle of $x$ or of $y$ avoids rows $1,2$, or contains both (a turn of
$C_x$ toggles the flippability of $(x,y)$ when $y\notin C_x$, and
turning a cycle through both marked rows leaves $\sigma$ unchanged).
Hence: in the \emph{together} state every move is available, including
every separating one; in the \emph{apart} state every move is
available except the splits of $C_1$, the splits of $C_2$, and the
merges of $C_1$ with $C_2$, which require flippability.  The merges
$C_1$--$C_2$ are the bit-changing moves.

(iii) \emph{Flippability, exactly.}  Let $a=L(x,j)$, $b'=L(y,j')$ and
$q^*$ the column where row $x$ holds $b'$ ($q^*\ne j$ when
$\pi(x)\ne y$).  Following the $\rho_{x,y}$-cycle of $j$, column $j'$
is reached exactly at the step after $q^*$; so $(x,y)$ is flippable iff
$q^*$ does not lie on the $\rho_{x,y}$-cycle of $j$.  For a random-like
$\rho_{x,y}$ this has probability $\tfrac12$, which is what is measured
($0.500$--$0.511$ in every class: separating, non-separating, merging,
other; and exactly $0$ for adjacent pairs).  A turn of $C_1$ (the move
$X\to J$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:trapped}) toggles the flippability
of \emph{every} merging pair simultaneously, so
$\Pr_X[\text{no flippable merge}\mid\text{apart}]\le2\Pr_J[\text{all merges flippable}]$;
this does not by itself show either is small.

What a proof must supply is therefore: (F1) under $\mathrm{Unif}(X)$,
in the apart state, a positive fraction of the pairs
$C_1\times C_2$ (and of the splitting pairs of $C_1$, $C_2$) are
flippable, except on a set of squares of measure $o(1/\log^2n)$; and
(F2) a mixing argument for the frame walk in which all moves are
available at rate $\asymp1/n^2$ except the constrained ones, which are
available at rate $\ge c/n^2$ on the event (F1).  Both are statements
about a pair of rows and a pair of columns of a random Latin square,
with no intercalates, orbits or expanders; (F1) is the kind of
statement the switching method reaches.  Measured on real squares
with true marks ($n=30$, $900$ instances, \texttt{runs/s13/trade30.log}):
flippable fraction $0$ for adjacent pairs and $0.5005$, $0.4999$ for
same-cycle and different-cycle pairs; the autocorrelation of the bit
after $m$ trades is $0.559,0.413,0.307,0.250,0.170,0.121,0.069,0.048,
0.025,0.007,0.004,0.002$ at $m=1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16,24,32,48,64$, against
$0.617,0.454,0.345,0.279,0.192,0.143,0.086,0.058,0.026,0.011,0.003,0.005$
for the walk with iid transpositions avoiding $u,v$ from a uniform
derangement frame: the chain decorrelates slightly faster (the
adjacency exclusion again) and reaches the noise floor by $m\approx32$,
with no plateau.  [With the symbol/column bug the same experiment
showed a plateau at $\approx0.09$: the frozen instances were exactly
those with $\pi(1)=2$ or $\pi(2)=1$, impossible for a true mark, on
which the chain --- which preserves $\pi^{-1}(1)$ and $\pi^{-1}(2)$
--- cannot move the bit.]
